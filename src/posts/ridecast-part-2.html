--- title: ridecast part 2: from warnings to decisions date: 2026-09-24 label: Sep 2026 ---

the first ridecast post ended with a map that knows the weather at my arrival time at every point, and a list of warnings: rain here, fog there, cold after dark. a list is not a decision, though. part 2 is about turning it into one: which road, when to leave, what to wear. it grows with every stage, like the first one.

which road is safer

OSRM usually gives one or two alternatives to the fastest route. stage 6 gets the forecast for each of them, one request per route, and scores it. every sample point already has a level from none to high; the score is the average over the route, weighted by how much of the route each point stands for, with weights 0, 1, 3 and 9. the steps grow fast on purpose: 20 km of heavy rain should count for more than 100 km of a damp road.

the route with the lowest score gets an "en güvenli rota" badge with what it costs against the fastest one, for example "+18 dk, risk %40 daha düşük". two rules keep the badge honest. if the safer route is less than 10% better, the fastest keeps it, because a detour for a rounding error is not advice. and if no route has any risk at all, there is no badge.

a dry day cannot show any of this, so I tested it the way I tested the break advice in part 1: I overrode fetch in the browser and put 3 mm/h of rain on every forecast point south of 40.6° N and west of 29.8° E. that covers the Gemlik and İznik part of the main route from Kadıköy to Eskişehir; the alternative through Sakarya and Bilecik stays outside it.

the badge said +29 minutes and 92% lower risk, which is 3:54 minus 3:25; the percentage comes from the unrounded scores, the table shows them rounded to one decimal. the whole comparison took two forecast requests. when I then changed the speed to 100 km/h, both routes were scored again (2 h 44 min and 3 h 7 min, +23 minutes) without a single new request, because the sample points depend only on the route and the forecast is cached by their coordinates; only the arrival times moved.

the score is my own weighting, and it measures the share of distance at each level, not the time spent there. a slow stretch in the rain counts the same as a fast one of the same length. that is the next thing I would change if the suggestions start to feel wrong.

when to leave

stage 7 adds a third departure option next to "now" and a fixed time: "en iyi saat". it takes every full hour in the next 24 hours, builds the whole timeline for each one (same speed, same breaks), scores the route the same way as in stage 6, and lists the three lowest, with the arrival time and the score. equal scores go to the earlier departure. the first one is taken automatically, and tapping another re-plans the route for that time.

24 candidates did not cost 24 requests. the sample points belong to the route, not to the departure, so all candidates need the forecast at the same coordinates, just for later hours. I made every forecast request ask for at least three days, which covers the last candidate's arrival, and the normal view and all 24 candidates now read one cached response. switching to "en iyi saat" on a real route made no new request at all.

in the rain test no morning hour made the list. even 15:00 is not clean: the wet road estimate remembers the last three hours of rain, so the start of the route still counts as wet. the real-forecast row shows a weakness too: on a calm day the three best are simply three neighbouring hours, which is correct but not very useful. spreading them out is a small change I left for later.

what to wear, where to stop

stage 8 has two small parts. the first is a clothing list. it is a fixed table, not a model: each row names an item, the vehicles it applies to, and a condition, like a felt temperature of 8° or less, rain or a wet road, darkness, or gusts of 50 km/h. the app takes the worst value of each along the route and prints every row that matches, with the reason next to it. on a real night run nine rows matched for the motorcycle, from a wind layer to heated gloves and a reflective vest (felt -1°, 1 km of fog, dark), and none for a car. two of the nine were visors, one for fog and one for the dark, saying almost the same thing, so they are now one item, and rows that name the same item merge their reasons instead of repeating it.

the second part finds fuel stations, motorway services and rest areas near the route, from OpenStreetMap through Overpass, and lets me add any of them as a break. my first query asked for everything within 1 km of the route line. Overpass answered with a 504, and on the retry it took 27 seconds and came back empty with a timeout note hidden in an otherwise normal response. so I measured the other way of asking: one bounding box per stretch of route, and throw away anything further than 1 km from the line in the browser.

the boxes return more than needed, and that is fine: 426 elements are nothing to filter locally. the app keeps one stop of each kind per 10 km (106 became 32 on this route), and where the forecast says rain, snow or real cold it drops the stops with no roof and marks the rest in blue. whether a stop has a roof is a guess from the tags: a fuel station has a canopy, motorway services have buildings, and a rest area only counts if it is tagged with a shelter, a shop or a building. the query only runs when I press the button, because Overpass is a shared server, and these times depend on how busy it is.

a trip you can send

stage 9 puts the whole trip into the address bar and keeps it there while I edit: the stops with a short label, the breaks and their minutes, the vehicle, the speeds, the departure and which route is selected. there is no server to save anything on, so the link is the save file. a trip with three breaks, road speeds and a fixed departure came to 253 characters, and opening it in a new tab gave the same arrival to the minute, the same breaks at the same kilometres and times, and the same alternative route. the weather is not in the link on purpose: it is fetched again when the link is opened, so whoever gets it sees the forecast of that moment.

a link is input from outside, so the decoder checks every field and returns nothing rather than half a trip: a latitude of 95, a speed of 900, a break of zero minutes and six other broken cases each have a test. a bad link shows a message and leaves the current trip as it was. trips are also kept in the browser as a short "son rotalar" list, which simply does not appear when the browser does not allow storage.

the copy button taught me something. in the automated browser I test with, the clipboard call never answered: no success, no error, the promise just stayed open, so the button did nothing visible. a real browser can do the same while it waits for a permission prompt. now the app waits 1.5 seconds and then shows the link in a field, already selected, ready to copy by hand.

riding with it

the last stage is the one meant for the bike: "yolculuğu başlat" follows the phone's GPS along the route and turns the screen into a view you can read at a glance, with the next warning and how far away it is, the time and distance left, and the weather at the next point. the arrival times do not stay those of the plan. the app compares how long the last 15 minutes of riding took with how long the plan said they would, and re-plans the rest of the trip from where I am at that pace. every 12 minutes it asks for a fresh forecast, and a warning ahead that is new or worse raises a banner and vibrates. three GPS fixes in a row more than 150 m off the route bring up "rotayı yeniden hesapla", which plans again from here to whatever is still ahead. the screen stays on through the Wake Lock API, and when the browser refuses that, the view says so.

I could not ride 273 km for a test, so the browser got a fake GPS: I replaced watchPosition with a stub and fed it points of the real route with made-up timestamps. 15 km in 10 minutes is 90 km/h against a plan of 80, a pace of 1.125, and the view showed 2 h 51 min left, which is the 3 h 13 min of planned riding divided by 1.125. the fake GPS found three real problems. after a reroute, the last fix of the old route was used to place me on the new one. the first forecast for the new route then flagged every warning on it as new. and warnings came back as "new" when their arrival time crossed an hour boundary, sometimes replacing a more serious alert that was still open. each has a fix now: fixes belong to one route, a new route's first forecast is only a baseline, and a warning is news once per route unless it gets worse.

a smaller lesson from the same test: the automated tab was in the background, where Chrome slows timers down to about one per second, so my test scripts that waited in small steps crawled and looked frozen. feeding the fixes without timers and waiting between steps from the outside fixed the testing, not the app.

what this does not show

nothing here has been tried on a real ride yet. on the web, GPS tracking is not reliable while the phone is locked or the browser is in the background, which is exactly how a phone often sits on a bike; that is the real argument for moving the pure core into a native app one day. the build goes on in ridecast part 3: a map that behaves, and longer trips. the risk thresholds are my own starting values, the road type and the wet road are guesses, and the free services it depends on (OSRM's demo server, Overpass, OpenStreetMap tiles) are meant for light use, not for many riders at once.
