ridecast part 2: from warnings to decisions

the first ridecast post ended with a map that knows the weather at my arrival time at every point, and a list of warnings: rain here, fog there, cold after dark. a list is not a decision, though. part 2 is about turning it into one: which road, when to leave, what to wear. it grows with every stage, like the first one.

which road is safer

OSRM usually gives one or two alternatives to the fastest route. stage 6 gets the forecast for each of them, one request per route, and scores it. every sample point already has a level from none to high; the score is the average over the route, weighted by how much of the route each point stands for, with weights 0, 1, 3 and 9. the steps grow fast on purpose: 20 km of heavy rain should count for more than 100 km of a damp road.

the route with the lowest score gets an "en güvenli rota" badge with what it costs against the fastest one, for example "+18 dk, risk %40 daha düşük". two rules keep the badge honest. if the safer route is less than 10% better, the fastest keeps it, because a detour for a rounding error is not advice. and if no route has any risk at all, there is no badge.

a dry day cannot show any of this, so I tested it the way I tested the break advice in part 1: I overrode fetch in the browser and put 3 mm/h of rain on every forecast point south of 40.6° N and west of 29.8° E. that covers the Gemlik and İznik part of the main route from Kadıköy to Eskişehir; the alternative through Sakarya and Bilecik stays outside it.

the badge said +29 minutes and 92% lower risk, which is 3:54 minus 3:25; the percentage comes from the unrounded scores, the table shows them rounded to one decimal. the whole comparison took two forecast requests. when I then changed the speed to 100 km/h, both routes were scored again (2 h 44 min and 3 h 7 min, +23 minutes) without a single new request, because the sample points depend only on the route and the forecast is cached by their coordinates; only the arrival times moved.

the score is my own weighting, and it measures the share of distance at each level, not the time spent there. a slow stretch in the rain counts the same as a fast one of the same length. that is the next thing I would change if the suggestions start to feel wrong.

when to leave

stage 7 adds a third departure option next to "now" and a fixed time: "en iyi saat". it takes every full hour in the next 24 hours, builds the whole timeline for each one (same speed, same breaks), scores the route the same way as in stage 6, and lists the three lowest, with the arrival time and the score. equal scores go to the earlier departure. the first one is taken automatically, and tapping another re-plans the route for that time.

24 candidates did not cost 24 requests. the sample points belong to the route, not to the departure, so all candidates need the forecast at the same coordinates, just for later hours. I made every forecast request ask for at least three days, which covers the last candidate's arrival, and the normal view and all 24 candidates now read one cached response. switching to "en iyi saat" on a real route made no new request at all.

in the rain test no morning hour made the list. even 15:00 is not clean: the wet road estimate remembers the last three hours of rain, so the start of the route still counts as wet. the real-forecast row shows a weakness too: on a calm day the three best are simply three neighbouring hours, which is correct but not very useful. spreading them out is a small change I left for later.
